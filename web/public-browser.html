Try it with your private GitHub repositories

    
Sign in to GitHub in the remote browser. Hand control back, and your Agent can count and list the private repositories owned by your account.

    

      
After connecting, send this task to your Agent:

      
Open GitHub in Agentic Browser and send me a link to sign in. Wait until I hand control back, then count all the private repositories I own and list their names. Check every page and close the browser when finished.

      
      

      
Enter your username, password, and verification codes in the remote browser. You do not need to send them to your Agent in chat.

    

    
You can also choose another public website. Each session has a separate temporary browser, starts with 5 minutes, and lasts at most 10 minutes. Concurrency and request limits apply. Login profiles are not saved. Websites such as Google and X may require verification or refuse sign-in.

    
The deployment administrator can manage the environment. After handback, your Agent can read the page. Keep your browser link private. Use a self-hosted instance for everyday work.

    
This HTTP connection does not encrypt the browser display or your input. The GitHub task is available over HTTPS. For now, use public pages and fictional data only.

  

  

    
Run your own instance

    
Use the public demo to try the handoff flow, then deploy a personal or team workspace on your own machine. Published images support Linux amd64 and arm64, including Linux containers on Apple Silicon Macs.

    
The images include Chrome, zkPass TransGate, and zkPass Schema Validator. Each session gets its own container; team members have separate accounts, credentials, and saved profiles.
